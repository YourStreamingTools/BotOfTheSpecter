3.3 Services you connect

If you connect a third-party service, we receive and store what is needed to make that feature work. These are the integrations in the product:

  • Spotify: OAuth tokens and account IDs for now-playing, song requests, and related music features.

  • YouTube VOD uploads: if you connect your YouTube channel so finished streams can be uploaded, we use Google OAuth 2.0 with the YouTube Data API v3. We ask for youtube.readonly to identify and show your connected channel and videos, and youtube.upload to upload videos you queue, or that are uploaded automatically when you have turned on auto-upload, using the title, description, and privacy setting you choose. We store your YouTube access and refresh tokens, channel ID and title, and upload job records on our servers so the stream server can upload for you. We do not share your tokens with other users. We do not sell, share, or use Google user data for advertising. If you disconnect YouTube, we revoke the Google token and delete the stored YouTube token record. You can also revoke access at any time from your Google Account, under Third-party access. BotOfTheSpecter's use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Each third-party service has its own privacy policy. We only ask for the access needed for the features you turn on, and you can disconnect integrations from the dashboard where that option is provided.

3.9 Specter Camera phone app (iOS and Android)

Specter Camera is our phone app. It sends your phone's camera and microphone to your own BotOfTheSpecter Cloud Studio, or to the BotOfTheSpecter Connector App on your own computer. This section explains what the app sends and stores.

  • Camera and microphone: the app asks for camera and microphone access, and only uses them to show the preview and to send live video and audio when you tap Go live. Video is sent as H.264 and audio as AAC. The camera is also used to scan link QR codes. You can mute the microphone in the app, and you can turn off either permission in your phone's settings.
  • Cloud route: video and audio are sent over an encrypted connection to our servers, which pass the stream through to your own Cloud Studio. Our servers do not record or store the stream, and do not forward it to Twitch or any other destination.
  • Local route: if you choose To: Local PC and have paired the app with the Connector App, video and audio go straight from your phone to your own computer over your local network and do not pass through our servers. The app asks for local network access on iPhone for this. It also asks the Connector App to confirm the camera name and local pairing key before it sends.
  • Linking your phone: when you link with a code from Cloud Studio, the app sends that one-time code and your phone's name, for example "Your iPhone", to api.botofthespecter.com. We issue a device link token for that phone and store it, together with the phone's name, against your BotOfTheSpecter account so Cloud Studio can show your linked phones. The app keeps the token in the iOS Keychain on iPhone or in Android's encrypted secure storage on Android, so your account API key is not stored on the phone. If you link by typing your BotOfTheSpecter API key instead, the key is kept in the same secure storage on that phone.
  • Link checks: when the app opens, before it goes live, and when a stream drops, it checks with our API (/v2/camera/device/me, using its device link token) that the phone is still linked. If it has been unlinked, the app forgets the link.
  • Stored on your phone: your stream quality choice, an install ID the app creates to identify that installation, the camera name, and the link described above. The install ID is also sent to the Connector App on the local route so it can tell phones apart.
  • Unlinking: you can unlink a phone in the app (Settings, then Unlink this phone) or in Cloud Studio (the Linked phones list). Unlinking removes the link so the phone can no longer stream to your account.
  • No tracking: the app does not use analytics, advertising, or tracking tools, does not use your location, contacts, or photos, and does not read your advertising identifier. The only third-party code it includes is a streaming library (HaishinKit on iPhone, RootEncoder on Android) and, on Android, a QR code scanning library (ZXing). These run on your phone and do not send data to their authors.

The usual technical data described in section 3.5, such as IP address and connection times, is also handled when the app connects to our servers.
